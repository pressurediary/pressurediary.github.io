How to measure blood pressure at home correctly

Small mistakes can change a reading by 10 mmHg or more. This checklist helps you get readings you and your doctor can trust.

Before you measure

  • No coffee, smoking or exercise in the 30 minutes before.
  • Empty your bladder.
  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes before the first reading.
  • Use an upper-arm monitor with a cuff that fits your arm. Wrist monitors are less reliable.

How to sit

  • Sit on a chair with your back supported and both feet flat on the floor. Do not cross your legs.
  • Rest your arm on a table so the cuff is at heart level.
  • Put the cuff on bare skin, about 2–3 cm above the elbow, not over a sleeve.
  • Do not talk or look at your phone while the monitor is measuring.

When and how often

  • Measure twice a day: in the morning before medication and breakfast, and in the evening.
  • Take 2 readings each time, 1 minute apart, and write down both.
  • Use the same arm every time. At the very first measurement, check both arms and then use the one with the higher value.
  • Before a doctor’s visit, measure for about 7 days in a row. Your doctor will look at the average, not at a single reading.

What the numbers mean

The first number (systolic) is the pressure when your heart beats; the second (diastolic) is the pressure between beats. Many guidelines, such as the European ones, consider an average home reading of 135/85 mmHg or higher as high. US guidelines use lower limits (130/80 mmHg). Your doctor decides what applies to you.

Call emergency services if your blood pressure is 180/120 mmHg or higher and you have chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness or numbness, trouble speaking or changes in vision.
